## 1. Core Conceptual Frameworks

### **Framework A: The "Feedback Loop" Concept**
**How users conceptualize it:**

**Transcription 28:**
> "It's for expanding the depth of reading. And I imagine it's to find out from your audience what topics are most of interest."

**Transcription 32:**
> "I think this feature helps you mark what you think that needs to be done, and that's always useful."

**Mental Model:**
```
Reader Experience → Identify Gap → Signal Need → Wikipedia Responds → Better Content
```

**Key Insight:** Users see this as a **demand-driven content system**. They're essentially "voting with their interest" - telling Wikipedia what matters.

**Conceptual Understanding:**
- This is a **two-way communication channel**, not just a one-way request
- It's about **prioritization**, not just content creation
- Users are **co-designers** of Wikipedia's content roadmap

---

### **Framework B: The "Expert Filter" Concept**
**How users think about authority and expertise:**

**Transcription 27:**
> "I would think it was for people who really knew what was going on and how to give more information... people that are knowledgeable or experts in the field."

**Transcription 27 (later clarifies):**
> "Like I said, if I'm not an expert, unless I'm an expert, I really wouldn't be trying to change anything or giving my opinion."

**Transcription 29:**
> "I wouldn't say I didn't find what I was looking for, but I was pretty satisfied with that."

**Mental Model:**
```
Expert Knowledge → Wikipedia Editorial → Verified Content → Reader Trust
```

**Key Insight:** There's a **conceptual tension** between:
1. **Feeling unqualified** to edit directly
2. **Knowing what information they need**

**The Paradox:**
- Users don't feel expert enough to *create* content
- But they DO feel expert enough to *identify gaps*
- This creates a psychological "safe zone" for contribution

**Quote (Transcription 34):**
> "I don't think there's many topics where I am suitably informed enough to amend someone else's work, unless they were writing about me."

**Conceptual Understanding:**
- Suggesting = **Consumer perspective** (I know what I want to buy)
- Editing = **Producer perspective** (I know how to make it)
- Users are comfortable as consumers, not producers

---

### **Framework C: The "Marketplace of Ideas" Concept**
**How users think about collective intelligence:**

**Transcription 28:**
> "I'd be confident you'd do something with it. There's no point canvassing people's opinion, it's more hassle for you if you're not gonna take it."

**Transcription 31:**
> "I think it's to get more information and to suggest content... I think that is useful. However, obviously with the development of AI and things like that, it's whether people would actually use that."

**Transcription 34:**
> "If I make it 34 times and I have the time to come back to the same page, I see that nothing happens. I think it would just discourage me."

**Mental Model:**
```
Many Readers → Aggregate Demand → Editorial Response → Collective Benefit
```

**Key Insight:** Users conceptualize this as **democratic content development**
- "If many people want it, it should happen"
- Crowdsourced prioritization
- The feature is a **voting mechanism**

**Conceptual Understanding:**
- They expect their individual voice to be **aggregated with others**
- They understand they're part of a **community signal**
- They expect **popularity to drive action**

**Quote (Transcription 32):**
> "I think it's when for articles that are not very much work, it can help locate the articles that collect interest in the people, so they are priority in working on them."

---

### **Framework D: The "Incomplete Product" Concept**
**How users view Wikipedia's completeness:**

**Two Opposing Views Emerged:**

**View 1: Wikipedia as "Good Enough"**

**Transcription 29:**
> "I've never got disappointed by Wikipedia before though. There's always some content... It's very good. I would trust it."

**View 2: Wikipedia as "Work in Progress"**

**Transcription 34:**
> "Some articles are really too short without any sections... if we're in a more niche or obscure page... I don't think that it will happen so soon."

**Mental Model (Spectrum):**
```
Complete Encyclopedia ←→ Living Document ←→ Incomplete Draft
```

**Key Insight:** Users hold **simultaneous, contradictory beliefs**:
1. "Wikipedia is comprehensive and trustworthy"
2. "Wikipedia has gaps I personally notice"

**Resolution Strategy:** Users rationalize this as:
- **Popular topics = complete**
- **Niche topics = incomplete**
- **My interests = often niche**

**Conceptual Understanding:**
- The feature **validates the "living document" mental model**
- It makes Wikipedia's incompleteness **acceptable** rather than problematic
- Users shift from "disappointed consumer" to "active participant"

---

### **Framework E: The "Black Box vs. Glass Box" Concept**
**Understanding of the editorial process:**

**Current State: BLACK BOX**

**Transcription 31:**
> "This is what I'm not sure about. It's been sent to the team, but I don't know what that means. I don't know who that means, I don't know the timescales."

**Transcription 32:**
> "I don't know how this works, like, if it goes to anyone, if it goes to a specific people, if you need to go and find this thing or if it's like some kind of ranking."

**Transcription 34:**
> "What questions does it raise for me is, are they really going to take this seriously and do what's been asked of them."

**Mental Models Users Are Building:**

**Model 1: Corporate Structure**
- "Wikipedia team" = employees
- "Administrators" = managers
- Centralized decision-making

**Model 2: Community Collaboration**
- "Editors" = volunteers
- "Moderators" = quality control
- Distributed creation

**Model 3: Hybrid/Uncertain**
- "I don't know if it's crowdsourced"
- "Maybe AI will do it"
- "Probably goes to validators"

**Key Insight:** **Nobody actually knows how it works**, and this creates:
- Uncertainty about effectiveness
- Anxiety about being ignored
- Desire for transparency

**What Users Want: GLASS BOX**
- See the process
- Understand the pipeline
- Track progress
- Know who's involved

**Quote (Transcription 34):**
> "There should be a way to incentivize the writers to go and write about specific contents here."

## 2. Conceptual Tensions & Contradictions

### **Tension 1: Contribution vs. Expertise**

**The Contradiction:**
- "I want to help, BUT I'm not qualified"
- "I know what's missing, BUT I don't know enough to add it"

**Resolution:** Suggestion feature as **"safe contribution"**
- Lower stakes than editing
- Consumer role vs. producer role
- Identification vs. creation

**Transcription 32:**
> "I think for editing, you need to follow very specific rules and get your sources correctly, and that's a work I can't do. But suggesting content if it's closed options, it's very easy."

---

### **Tension 2: Trust vs. Skepticism**

**The Contradiction:**
- "Wikipedia is always accurate" (general trust)
- "My suggestion probably won't be implemented" (specific skepticism)

**Mental Model:**
```
Trust in CONTENT ≠ Trust in PROCESS
```

**Transcription 31:**
> "How much do you trust that your content requests would lead to improvements in the article? I wouldn't really trust... maybe more like 10%."

**Transcription 29:**
> "I would trust it [Wikipedia content]" BUT "I'd say 30-40-50% confident [about suggestions being implemented]"

**Key Insight:** Users separate **output quality** from **input responsiveness**

---

### **Tension 3: Effort vs. Value**

**The Calculation Users Make:**

**Transcription 31:**
> "I think nothing would stop me using it because you've got nothing to lose, BUT that's not an option."

**Transcription 34:**
> "If I make it 34 times and I see that nothing happens, I think it would just discourage me."

**Mental Formula:**
```
Will I Use This? = (Perceived Impact / Effort Required) × Probability of Success
```

**Currently:**
- **Effort:** Low (few clicks)
- **Impact:** Unknown
- **Probability:** Low (30-50%)
- **Result:** Maybe use once, unlikely to repeat

**What Would Change the Formula:**
- Show that others requested same thing (increase perceived impact)
- Show content was added (increase probability)
- Make process transparent (reduce uncertainty)

---

### **Tension 4: Preset vs. Custom**

**Deep Conceptual Issue:**

Users see **two fundamentally different types of gaps:**

**Type A: "I don't know what I don't know"**
- Presets work perfectly here
- "Oh, I didn't realize I could learn about bean varieties!"
- Discovery-oriented

**Type B: "I know exactly what's missing"**
- Presets are frustrating here
- "I want to know about Mars colonization plans by SpaceX specifically"
- Goal-oriented

**Transcription 34:**
> "I want more details about the planned mission to Mars that Elon Musk is trying to make happen with SpaceX. I don't think it has been brought up here, so I want, I'm wondering if we can just suggest that."

**Key Insight:** The feature needs to support **two cognitive modes:**
1. **Exploration mode** - "What could I learn?"
2. **Search mode** - "Where's the specific thing I need?"

## 3. Deeper Mental Model Analysis

### **Concept: "The Suggestion as Signal"**

Users think of suggestions not as requests but as **data points**:

**Transcription 26:**
> "I think it's a compromise between giving you a whole encyclopedia article when you just wanted to know one thing."

**Transcription 28:**
> "I think you're asking for it and Wikipedia is my go to place, I'd be confident you'd do something with it."

**Mental Model:**
- My suggestion = 1 vote
- Many votes = priority queue
- High votes = action

**This is actually a **PRODUCT ANALYTICS mindset**:**
- Users understand data-driven decisions
- They expect aggregation
- They want to see the aggregate data

**What This Means:**
Users would respond well to seeing:
- "47 people requested more about cultivation"
- "This is the 3rd most requested topic this week"
- "Last updated based on user suggestions: 2 days ago"

---

### **Concept: "The Time Gap Problem"**

**The Invisible Timeline:**

**Transcription 31:**
> "I would say give a timeline within that suggestion, say if you request pictures, that could take 10 weeks. If you request texts, that would take 5 working days."

**Mental Model of Time:**
```
Digital Request → ??? → Digital Response
```

**User Expectation (unconscious):**
- Digital systems = fast response
- "Send" button = something happens now
- No response = broken or ignored

**Reality of Wikipedia:**
- Volunteer editors
- Research takes time
- Quality control processes
- Not automated

**The Disconnect:**
Users are applying **software expectations** to a **human-powered system**

**Quote (Transcription 34):**
> "I'm really dubious about the speed at which this is going to be updated."

**Conceptual Fix Needed:**
- Explicitly set human-time expectations
- Show the human process behind the curtain
- Celebrate when things DO get updated

---

### **Concept: "The Contribution Ladder"**

Users conceptualize a **hierarchy of contribution:**

```
Level 1: Read (consume)
   ↓
Level 2: Suggest (signal)
   ↓
Level 3: Edit (create)
   ↓
Level 4: Moderate (validate)
```

**Key Finding:** Users see the suggestion feature as **"Level 1.5"** - a stepping stone

**Transcription 28:**
> "The interface for us is to expand information but only put information where there's a demand for it... you are receiving this information to make informed decisions about where to expand."

**Conceptual Understanding:**
- This isn't just a feature
- It's a **gateway to deeper participation**
- It's **onboarding to Wikipedia culture**

**Strategic Implication:**
Some users who suggest today might edit tomorrow. The feature is actually:
- **Reducing intimidation**
- **Building familiarity**
- **Creating investment**

**Quote (Transcription 34):**
> "Maybe Wikipedia is trying also to get readers to engage and maybe start becoming editors by suggesting topics they can write on."

---

### **Concept: "The Legitimacy Question"**

**Deep underlying concern:** "Who am I to say what should be in Wikipedia?"

**Transcription 25:**
> "How much genuinity of that information can be sometimes questioned. But that Wikipedia will take care of that I know."

**Transcription 27:**
> "Unless I was an expert in the field, I wouldn't use it... if you're not an expert, I don't think you should be messing with other people's stuff."

**Mental Model:**
```
My Opinion < Expert Knowledge < Wikipedia Standards
```

**The Permission Problem:**
Users need **psychological permission** to make suggestions

**What Grants Permission:**
1. "Others requested this too" (social proof)
2. "Wikipedia asked for my input" (explicit invitation)
3. "My suggestion helps editors" (framing as assistance)
4. "I'm identifying, not creating" (role clarity)

**Transcription 33:**
> "Very useful for both us as readers and for the people that are doing the research and putting out these kind of articles."

**Key Insight:** Frame suggestions as **"helping the helpers"** not "criticizing Wikipedia"

## 5. Deep Conceptual Insights

### **Insight 1: The "Democratization Frame"**

**What Users Are Really Asking For:**
Not just content - they want **participation in Wikipedia's governance**

**Evidence:**
- They care about WHO handles suggestions
- They want to see aggregated demand
- They expect transparent processes
- They suggest incentive systems

**Deeper Meaning:**
The feature represents **reader empowerment** - shifting from passive consumption to active input

**Quote (Transcription 28):**
> "Should there be an open source for me to put what I think needs to be changed or improved upon?"

---

### **Insight 2: The "Trust Transfer"**

**Conceptual Phenomenon:**
Users trust Wikipedia's **content quality** and want to extend that trust to **process quality**

**The Logic:**
```
If: Wikipedia produces good content
And: Wikipedia is asking for my input
Then: Wikipedia has a good process for handling input
```

**The Problem:**
Users don't actually know if that third line is true

**Quote (Transcription 29):**
> "I've never got disappointed by Wikipedia before... I would trust it."

**Conceptual Fix:**
Make the process **as trustworthy as the content**
- Show quality control
- Display editorial standards
- Prove suggestions matter

---

### **Insight 3: The "Custom vs. Guided" Spectrum**

**Deep Conceptual Divide:**

```
Fully Guided              Hybrid              Fully Custom
(Presets only)      (Presets + text)      (Free-form only)
     ↓                     ↓                      ↓
Easy but limited    Best of both         Flexible but harder
```

**What Users Actually Want:** **THE MIDDLE**

**Evidence:**
- T28 wanted presets BUT with open field
- T32 wanted both approaches combined
- T34 wanted presets for speed, custom for specificity

**The Concept:**
**"Scaffolded Freedom"** - guidance when you need it, flexibility when you don't

**Design Implication:**
```
Start: Preset options (easy entry)
   ↓
Expand: "Something else?" (transition)
   ↓
Open: Free text field (full flexibility)
```

---

### **Insight 4: The "Agency Restoration"**

**Fundamental Psychological Need:**

When users find gaps in Wikipedia, they experience **loss of agency**:
- "I need information"
- "It's not here"
- "I can't get it"
- "I can't fix it"

**Quote (Transcription 34):**
> "I've had this frustration of content not being there for specific reads... if I can suggest what I wanna see, it could spark maybe a little change."

**The Suggestion Feature = Agency Restoration**
- From helpless → helpful
- From frustrated → productive
- From excluded → included

**Conceptual Understanding:**
This isn't about features - it's about **psychological empowerment**

---

### **Insight 5: The "Asynchronous Contribution" Concept**

**Mental Model Users Are Building:**

```
Traditional Model:
  See gap → Research → Write → Submit → Review → Published
  (Time: Hours/Days, Skill: High)

New Model:
  See gap → Suggest → [Someone else does the work] → Check back
  (Time: 30 seconds, Skill: None)
```

**What This Enables:**
**"Micro-contributions"** - tiny acts that aggregate to massive value

**Quote (Transcription 34):**
> "It just takes a few seconds to shape the future of Wikipedia."

**Conceptual Framing:**
This is **"crowdsourcing content direction"** not "crowdsourcing content creation"

## 7. Critical Conceptual Gaps

### **Gap 1: Role Clarity**
- Users don't know if they're customers, contributors, or critics
- No clear identity provided by the feature

### **Gap 2: Process Visibility**
- Black box operation creates anxiety
- No mental model of how Wikipedia works internally

### **Gap 3: Value Proposition**
- Unclear what user gets out of suggesting
- No immediate gratification
- No long-term tracking

### **Gap 4: Social Context**
- Feel alone in their suggestions
- Don't see the collective effort

### **Gap 5: Authority Justification**

## 8. Design Principles Based on Conceptual Understanding

### **Principle 1: Make the Implicit Explicit**
Show what happens behind the scenes:
- "Your suggestion joins 47 others"
- "Average response time: 3 weeks"
- "This section was updated based on user requests"

### **Principle 2: Create Agency Through Transparency**
Users need to see they matter:
- Track their suggestions
- Show impact when it happens
- Acknowledge their contribution

### **Principle 3: Bridge the Expertise Gap**
Frame suggesting as legitimate participation:
- "You don't need to be an expert to identify gaps"
- "Readers help guide editors"
- "Your perspective matters"

### **Principle 4: Support Dual Cognitive Modes**
Enable both exploration and precision:
- Presets for discovery
- Custom text for specificity
- Easy switch between modes

### **Principle 5: Build Trust Through Consistency**
Extend content trust to process trust:
- Show editorial standards for suggestions
- Display quality control measures
- Prove the system works

## 9. The "Mental Model Mismatch" Problem

### **What Users Think:**
"I'm telling Wikipedia what content to create"

### **What's Actually Happening:**
"I'm adding data to a suggestion database that volunteer editors may or may not act on"

### **The Mismatch Creates:**
- Disappointment when nothing happens
- Confusion about the process
- Lowered trust in the feature

### **The Fix:**
**Align mental models with reality** through:
1. Honest communication about the volunteer model
2. Clear expectations about timelines
3. Celebration when it DOES work
4. Transparency about the challenges

## Conclusion: Conceptual Recommendations

To align the feature with user mental models:

1. **Frame it as "Reader Advisory Board"** - positions users as advisors
2. **Show the suggestion→editor→content pipeline** visually
3. **Display collective demand** ("You + 46 others")
4. **Provide status tracking** (received → reviewed → in progress → completed)
5. **Celebrate wins** ("This section was updated based on suggestions!")
6. **Set realistic expectations** ("Volunteer editors respond within 2-8 weeks")
7. **Make the volunteer model explicit** ("Help our volunteer editors prioritize")
8. **Enable both preset + custom** (scaffolded freedom)
9. **Show trending suggestions** ("Most requested this week")
10. **Create suggestion history page** (your personal impact dashboard)

**The Ultimate Goal:**
Transform users from **passive consumers** to **active community members** through a feature that makes contribution feel **meaningful, transparent, and rewarding**.
